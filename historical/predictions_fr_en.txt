Scientists have just highlighted how dog tail movements are related to his mood.
Previous studies had shown that happy dogs moved their tails more to the right (from the dog's point of view), while nervous dogs moved them more to the left.
But now scientists say dogs can detect and respond to these subtle differences.
Professor Georgio Vallortigara, a neuroscientist at the University of Trento, said: "Everyone knows that in man, the left and right sides of the brain are involved in different ways in stimuli that awaken positive or negative emotions."
Here we tried to see what was happening in other species.
He added that, as in humans, the right side of the brain was responsible for movement to the left and vice versa, and that both hemispheres played a different role in emotions.
To learn more about how dogs react to the disorderly tail movements of other dogs, researchers followed animals watching movies featuring other dogs.
They measured the heart rate of animals and analyzed their behaviour.
It will probably not take long before we understand why their tail sometimes goes on one side and sometimes on the other.
Professor Vallortigara explained: "We have presented to dogs films with other dogs – either a naturalistic version or a silhouette to avoid any other confusion – and we have been able to trade the tail movement and present it as going more left or right."
When the animals saw a dog with no expression moving the tail to the right (from the point of view of the dog moving the tail), they remained perfectly relaxed.
But when they spotted a tail that was mainly left-hand (again from the dog's point of view that was moving the tail), their heart rate accelerated and they looked anxious.
Professor Vallortigara said he did not think dogs were deliberately communicating with each other through these movements.
Instead, he thinks dogs know from experience the movements they should or should not worry about.
He added: "If you meet other dogs several times and you notice that, most of the time, the movement of their tail to one side is associated with a more friendly attitude and the right side leads to a less friendly attitude, you will react according to this experience."
Researchers indicate that the results could allow owners, veterinarians and trainers to better understand the emotions of their animals.
Canine behavior expert John Bradshaw, a visiting professor at the University of Bristol School of Veterinary Sciences, said this was not the first study to examine the importance of the left and right in the canine species.
Last year, a team from Lincoln University found that dogs were turning their heads to the left when looking at an aggressive dog and to the right when it was a happy dog.
And in another scientific study conducted by the University of Victoria in Canada, he said: "Dogs were more easily moved to a robot dog when its tail moved to the left rather than to the right, instead of becoming anxious – the opposite of what is in the Italian study."
He said that the differences could be due to the fact that the dogs who participated in the various studies did not fully consider the animals of the films or robot dogs as dogs.
A study of how dogs react to real dogs could be useful, he explained.
"Although many elements, found in different mammals, indicate that both sides of the brain are used for different purposes, many details still need to be clarified – and dogs are no exception," he said.
However, given the ease with which their behaviour can be recorded, it will probably not take long before we understand why their tail sometimes moves on one side and sometimes on the other.

Former CNN correspondent Pat Etheridge is a journalist specializing in family and child health issues.
Does a mother have to worry if her daughter's breasts and pubic hair start growing at 7 or 8 years old?
At the annual conference of the American Academy of Pediatrics held this week in Orlando, Florida, Dr.Paul Kaplowitz, a pediatric endocrinologist, explained that these early physical changes are quite common among young American women and represent a new standard.
"I spend a lot of time reassuring parents – in general, this does not indicate a rapid evolution towards full puberty," said Kaplowitz.
The obvious signs of development, such as breast budding, hair under the arms and in the pubic area and body odours, appear earlier in girls.
But there has been only a slight advance in the age of the first rules over the last four decades.
In the United States, the average age was 121⁄2 years, compared to 121⁄2 years in 1970.
"Once the chest has started to develop, it takes at least two or three years before the first rules appear," said Kaplowitz, also the author of "Early Puberty in Girls: The Essential Guide to Coping with This Common Problem."
Time is the most accurate test to know how puberty will evolve.
There is a debate about what constitutes the actual appearance of puberty, but it is considered "early" when the development of the chest is accompanied by sudden growth before the age of 8.
In most cases, development slows or stops – this is something that a pediatrician can follow closely.
A faster evolution may justify tests by an endocrinologist to rule out serious problems such as tumours or cysts.
There are treatments to delay the onset of the first period and prevent other consequences: premature bone aging that can slow growth and small size in adulthood.
The recommendations for drug and hormonal treatment are based on age of the child, developmental rate, growth rate and emotional maturity.
Psychosocial aspects are also important.
Kaplowitz is careful with the drugs but recognizes that "controlling puberty can alleviate some behavioural problems and the feeling for girls to be different from others."
The other big problem is understandable: Parents simply don't want their very young girls to have their menstruation.
"They fear the risk of pregnancy or even how they will manage hygiene issues," said Kaplowitz.
"It was a shock," recalls a woman whose daughter had her first period at the age of 10.
Even if there were signs and we had talked about the rules, it was not emotionally prepared.
She came home from school scared and upset to be the first among her friends.

Yet there is no consistent theory that hormones in milk or other foods, chemicals in the environment, or sexual messages from the media are responsible.
Boys, like girls, reach puberty earlier.
Kaplowitz says the most credible hypothesis is the obesity epidemic.
He participated in a study of girls aged 6 to 9 years that links the percentage of fat mass to the onset of puberty.
Other findings support this conclusion, but there are many other influential factors.

Globally, early puberty mechanisms appear to be influenced by everything, from economic conditions to climate, through genes.

Acne and pubic hair are common even in infants and toddlers.
